Dengue Spraying Ramps Up This Week
Hillsborough is the center of Florida's dengue outbreak, with 259 of the 296 cases reported statewide, according to Bay News 9. Health officials call it the largest dengue outbreak in any state since the 1940s.
Through Oct. 16, expect low-flying helicopters over Bloomingdale, Palm River, East Tampa and East Lake. They're treating standing water with Bti, a naturally occurring bacterium the county says doesn't harm people, pets, fish or birds. The county's local state of emergency runs through Oct. 15.
Why it matters: the mosquito that spreads dengue can breed in water as small as a bottle cap, so much of the fix is in your own yard. Dump or cover standing water, wear repellent, and if you're a county Solid Waste customer you can drop off up to 12 old tires a year for free.
This week's treatment areas (Hillsborough County): https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2026/10/11/daytime-mosquito-treatment-by-air-this-week-in-bloomingdale-palm-river-east-tampa-and-east-lake

Tampa Theatre Turns 100 This Week
Tampa Theatre's centennial week marks the end of a restoration that started in March to bring the 1926 movie palace back to its 1920s glory.
Tonight it's The Wizard of Oz at 7 p.m. ($13). Wednesday is A Night at the Opera (1935), also 7 p.m. and $13. Thursday's 100th birthday party, with the Mighty Wurlitzer scoring the 1924 silent comedy Sherlock Jr., is sold out.
See the centennial lineup: https://www.tampatheatre.org/

Early Voting Starts Monday
Early voting in Hillsborough runs Oct. 19 through Nov. 1, and vote-by-mail ballots are already going out. The ballot includes County Commission races and Amendment 3, which would raise the homestead exemption on non-school property taxes from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.
Axios' 3-minute Hillsborough voting guide: https://www.axios.com/local/tampa-bay/2026/10/08/3-minute-voting-guide-hillsborough-s-midterm-election

DEVELOPMENT WATCH

SoHo Stormwater Fight Heads to Court
Status: Approved by City Council (4-3, September). Now challenged in court.
Bern's Steak House and other South Howard Avenue property owners have filed two class-action lawsuits to halt the city's SoHo stormwater project. The plan runs 6,500 feet of box culverts under South Howard to carry floodwater from Palma Ceia Pines and Parkland Estates into Hillsborough Bay. Work is expected to take more than three years.
One suit says the culvert goes beyond the city's legal right to use the street. The other says the scope and cost changed without proper notice: 2023 bond documents estimated $76 million, while the first phase alone is priced at about $100 million. Why it matters: it's a fix for chronic flooding that means years of disruption in one of Tampa's busiest business districts.
WUSF's full story: https://www.wusf.org/courts-law/2026-10-12/berns-soho-property-owners-sue-tampa-massive-stormwater-project

Rays Stadium Bonds Up Thursday
Status: On Thursday's City Council agenda (Item 90).
City Council's Thursday, Oct. 15 session (9 a.m.) includes a vote to authorize up to $80 million in non-ad valorem revenue bonds for the city's Rays stadium contribution and start the required court validation. A city memo says actually issuing debt would need a later council vote.
The Tampa Monitor's breakdown: https://tampamonitor.com/news/rays-bonds-property-taxes-cant-repay/

Pendry Tampa Tops Out
Status: Under construction, opening next year.
Pendry Tampa wrapped vertical construction last week at 38 stories: 218 hotel rooms, 190 condos and ground-floor dining off the Riverwalk. Condos start at $1.7 million, and more than 80% are sold, according to a news release cited by Axios.
Photos from inside (Axios): https://www.axios.com/local/tampa-bay/2026/10/13/pendry-tampa-luxury-high-rise-vertical-construction
